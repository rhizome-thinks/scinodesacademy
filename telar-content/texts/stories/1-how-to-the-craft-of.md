---
title: "The Craft of Research (2008), Booth et al."
---

In *The Craft of Research (2008)*, Wayne Booth, Gregory Colomb and Joseph Williams make a useful distinction between an interest, a topic, and eventually a question.

An **interest** is what draws you in.
A **topic** is the part of that interest you decide to study.
A **research question** is what you want to find out about that topic.

**Interest**: 
Gives you a starting point for exploration. You're curious about a subject, and preliminary reading helps you see which aspects you might pursue (Booth et al., 2008, pp. 36 – 38).

**Topic**
Defines a subject you can study in depth. Try focusing on a particular action or relationship, which gives you something to investigate (Booth et al., 2008, pp. 36, 39 – 40).

**Research question**
Identifies something specific you want to understand about the topic. It guides you toward the evidence you need to develop and support an answer (Booth et al., 2008, pp. 40 – 41).

The move from **interest to topic** is about deciding what to study.
The move from **topic to question** is about deciding what you want to understand about it.

Remember, several questions can come out of the same focused topic (Booth et al., 2008, p. 41).
